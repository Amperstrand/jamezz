## The story (told in the first 20 seconds)

Alice is debanked. She lives in a state that wants to know what everyone
buys and how they move. Cash makes her a suspect; cards make her a ledger
entry. She will not missionary merchants into accepting bitcoin — she has
more important things to do. **She just wants to eat.**

## The flow we explain (20s–55s, explainer frames)

1. Alice scans **Burgermeister's own table QR** — the one the venue already
   printed. Merchant did nothing, converted nothing.
2. The secret sauce is **public**: a set of prompts that lets Alice **build
   a module — or commission one**. The prompts reverse the venue's API and
   checkout, create an account if needed with **cashu.email**, and the
   module is published in minutes.
3. **Bob** runs a bridge — maybe for many shops. He picks up the module and
   **announces on nostr**: for all intents and purposes, *I am
   Burgermeister* — I accept orders, I deliver food. **Bob now IS
   Burgermeister.** Alice never talks to the real restaurant; menu,
   checkout, and confirmation all come from Bob.
5. Burgermeister does not consent — doesn't even know Alice is a wanted
   freedom fighter. That's the point: freedom fighters have human rights
   too, and food is a human right.
6. Alice loads Bob in her **numo terminal**; she and her co-conspirators
   each check out, each pay over **Lightning**; Bob places the real order;
   Alice gets an **order number** screenshot for pickup.

## The insights (2:30–3:10, worded tight)

1. **Any action you can trigger through a proprietary API — order at the
   table, charge a car, pre-order electronics for pickup in an hour —
   becomes a set of prompts, built in minutes, then plain API calls.** The
   AIs are smart enough. Demand-driven: one person pays to build or fix a
   build or fix a bridge once, everyone benefits forever. Thousands of
   merchants a day, no permission asked, none needed.
2. **The POS multiplication effect.** We never target restaurants one by
   one — we target their POS integration. Jamezz alone: 2,000+ venues,
   25+ countries. One integration = hundreds of points on a city's map,
   for free. That's why the catalog already carries 75 live-verified
   tables.

## Production & live-day plan

### The audience QR is now the real demo

The video is the talk; the QR is the product. **A dead QR on stage is the
worst failure mode** — so: deploy the mock-mode live surface (landing page
with numo + Bob preloaded beats a raw worker URL), generate the QR from a
stable short link, deploy **before** the talk, leave it running all day,
test-scan from the back of the room with two phones. Mock-mode guard + rate
limit on the public surface: no real orders today, no runaway costs.

### The liveness question (pre-decided answer)

*"This cut was rehearsed for reliability; the flow is running live all day —
scan the code and watch it work."* The mock cut stays honest — no fake LIVE
badge, no fake timestamps. The QR makes the liveness claim true where it
counts.

### Rig + rehearsal

- Local files on the stage laptop + USB + second device; fullscreen player,
  no up-next; volume pre-set; HDMI + audio tested in the room.
- Rehearse narration against the cut with a stopwatch until ≤ 4:00.
- Rehearse the QR beat separately — it must hold long enough to scan.

Hard Q&A, pre-answered:

- **"Without consent?"** — Public QR, public API, venue paid full price in
  fiat, never sees Alice. No card data exists anywhere we run. The open
  web has always worked this way — the most based thing in the room is
  also the oldest idea: peer-to-peer cash over an open network.
- **"Custodial Bob?"** — Yes, non-atomic and custodial, said on stage.
  Ring signatures let Alice price that risk against keys she pinned
  herself — a risk signal, not a promise.
- **"What if the API changes?"** — Every drift becomes a fixture, a test,
  and a Lesson (jamezz#5). One customer pays for the fix; everyone keeps
  eating.
- **"Delivery?"** — Pickup today; jamezz#14 decides if delivery is one
  flag away.
