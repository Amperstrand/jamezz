# Spoken script — word-for-word (~510 words, 4:00, SINGLE SPEAKER)

One speaker throughout. Alice and Bob live **on screen** (labeled terminal
footage / title cards), never as voices. Bracketed lines are cues, not
spoken. If over time, cut from the honesty beat and the POS paragraph —
never from the story, the order run, or the prompt reveal.

---

**[0:00 — ALICE card: silhouette + table QR photo]**

Alice is debanked. She lives in a state that wants to know what everyone
buys and how everyone moves. Cash makes her a suspect. Cards make her a
ledger entry. She will not stand in a restaurant explaining bitcoin to a
merchant — she has more important things to do. She just wants to eat.

**[0:20 — explainer: QR → prompts → module → nostr announcement]**

So she scans the restaurant's own QR code — the one already printed on the
table. The merchant did nothing. The secret sauce is public: a set of prompts
that lets Alice build a module — or commission one. The prompts reverse the
venue's API and checkout, create an account when needed with cashu.email,
and a module is published in minutes.

**[0:45 — BOB card → bridge view]**

Bob runs a bridge — maybe one shop, maybe a hundred. He picks up the module,
and announces on nostr: for all intents and purposes, *I am Burgermeister* —
I accept orders, I deliver food. From this moment, Alice never talks to the
real restaurant. It never consented, never learns she exists — and still
gets paid in full, in fiat.

**[0:55 — ORDER RUN: ALICE-labeled numo terminal, mock-realistic]**

Alice loads Bob into her numo terminal. The real menu — live prices from
the API. She and her co-conspirators each build a cart; everyone pays their
own. Checkout: Lightning invoice… paid. Bob's bridge takes the sats,
charges the venue through the card rail, and places the order. Somewhere in
Berlin, a kitchen fires a ticket — and Alice gets her number to show at the
counter.

**[1:50 — PROMPT REVEAL: actual `prompts/onboard-table.md` scrolling on screen]**

Here is one of the actual prompts we're submitting. It reads a QR and
writes the integration. And notice the last line: *stop at the card page* —
the safety boundary is written into the prompt itself.

Because this is not a burger app. This is an **agentic framework that
onboards a million merchants without consent.** Any action you can trigger
through a proprietary API — order at the table, charge a car, pre-order
electronics for pickup in an hour — becomes a set of prompts, built in
minutes, then just API calls. The AIs are smart enough. And it's
demand-driven: one person pays to build or fix a module once — everyone
benefits forever.

**[2:40 — map animation: POS logos → hundreds of pins]**

And we never onboard restaurants one by one — we onboard their POS. One
platform like this sits under two thousand venues in twenty-five countries.
One integration lights up hundreds of points on a city's map, for free.
That's why seventy-five tables are already live-verified.

**[3:10 — honesty beat: bridge diagram, "non-atomic custodial" label]**

Now the honest part. Bob is custodial — we say it out loud because it's
true, and because ring signatures let Alice pin her own trust set and price
that risk herself. And there is no card data anywhere — not in the repos,
not in these videos, not ever. The person types their own card.

**[3:40 — CLOSE: audience QR, big, holds through the end]**

This QR is live for the rest of the day — scan it, load Bob, order lunch.

No permission was asked. None is needed. Food is a human right. Freedom of
movement is a human right. Alice just wanted to eat — and now, she can.

**[3:55 — end card: order number + logo]**
